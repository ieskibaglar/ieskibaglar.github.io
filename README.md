Where It All Started

From Electrical Engineering to OT Cybersecurity — Chapter 01

First, let me introduce myself.

I graduated in 2023 with a degree in Electrical and Electronics Engineering from Fırat University, located in eastern Türkiye.

Like many engineers at the beginning of their careers, I found myself facing a wide range of possible career paths. Electrical and electronics engineering is an incredibly broad field, and discovering where my abilities, interests, and curiosity truly belonged was not something that happened overnight.

My journey toward Operational Technology (OT) cybersecurity began with a few simple questions.

The interesting thing about those questions was that every answer seemed to lead to even bigger questions.

Although I had previously worked with companies involved in electrical power distribution and building electrical projects, a turning point in my professional journey came when I joined a thermal power plant in Türkiye as an Operations Engineer.

Working in a power plant exposed me to an entirely different level of industrial complexity.

Every day, I encountered interconnected systems, control processes, instrumentation, and equipment operating together to produce electricity. The more I observed these systems, the more I wanted to understand not only how they worked, but also what could happen if something went wrong.

And eventually, my curiosity took a different direction.

The Questions That Changed My Perspective

You might be wondering what kind of questions I was asking myself.

Consider a thermal power plant, a facility that plays a critical role in electricity generation.

Physical and occupational safety risks can be addressed through engineering design, protective systems, procedures, and regulations.

But what about threats we cannot see?

What could be the physical consequences of a malicious cyberattack entering an industrial environment through its IT or OT networks?

I began imagining scenarios.

What if a cyberattack disrupted a critical circulation system while combustion was still taking place, potentially causing severe thermal damage to a boiler?

What if turbine control functions were maliciously manipulated, creating dangerous operating conditions before the generator could be synchronized with the electrical grid?

Or perhaps something even more deceptive:

What if the sensor values displayed to operators appeared completely normal, convincing everyone that the process was under control, while the actual physical conditions had already exceeded critical safety limits?

These were hypothetical scenarios, but the underlying question was real.

Could a digital attack lead to a physical industrial disaster?

The more I thought about it, the more seriously I began to consider the relationship between cybersecurity and industrial safety.

I could imagine countless other possibilities, but my purpose here is not to describe ways of attacking industrial facilities.

It is to explain why protecting them became so important to me.

From Fear to Curiosity, and From Curiosity to Purpose

At first, the potential consequences of these scenarios genuinely frightened me.

But eventually, that concern turned into a different question:

What could I do to help prevent something like this from happening?

That was the moment my curiosity began turning into a professional ambition.

I wanted to understand how industrial control systems communicate, how their networks are structured, what makes them vulnerable, and how they can be protected without compromising operational reliability or safety.

Of course, deciding to follow this path was much easier than knowing where to begin.

Even today, there are still organizations where the distinction between Information Technology (IT) and Operational Technology (OT) is not fully understood, even at the organizational level.

There was no obvious roadmap in front of me.

I knew that I had a great deal to learn.

But I also knew that I had to start somewhere.

An Unexpected Opportunity

Then something happened that I had not anticipated.

Through a fortunate coincidence, I received an official notice informing me that I was being transferred to the plant's Automation Department.

What initially seemed like an ordinary change in professional responsibilities would become an important turning point in my journey.

I was about to move closer to the very systems I had been questioning.

And that is where the real story begins.
